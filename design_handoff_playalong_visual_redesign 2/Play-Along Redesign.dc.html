Turn 3 — the legato pair
Two pills, one body — and arcs that spring from the centre
Two corrections to Turn 2. The bridge is gone: a legato pair now butts flush at full height and its own corner radii do the work, which is what makes the phrase read as one gesture rather than two stops with a connector. And the hammer-on / pull-off arc is re-anchored to pill centres, so it no longer looks thrown forward off the leading edges. Both ship in the updated highway-theme.default.json.
3a · Flush legato join 3b · Centre-anchored arc 3c · Renderer notes
3a
The legato join is a flush butt, not a bridge — this ships
A bridge is a third object: you read pill, connector, pill. Take it away and set the two pills edge to edge at full height, and the corner radii they already have pinch the seam into two small notches. One continuous body, one outline, and the notch is the only thing that tells you where the second note starts — which is exactly the weight that reading deserves. The H or P still says what to do; the merged body says and it is one phrase. Against a 9px nick there is now no confusion possible: nick shows ground the full height of the pill, legato shows ground nowhere but the two notches.

3b
The arc springs from pill centres
On the built stage the arc's feet sit on the pills' leading edges, so the whole mark is shoved half a pill to the right — the apex lands past the seam, over the second note, and the phrase looks like it starts where it ends. The guides mark the two pill centres; the built arc misses both. Anchor each foot to the horizontal centre of its pill instead: the arc is then symmetrical about the pair, the apex sits above the notch, and the letter has an obvious home. It also makes the mark's width mean something — a wider arc is a wider interval in time, not an accident of which edges happened to be nearest.

3c
What changes in the renderer
1 · Delete the bridge. bridgeStraightRatio and bridgeInkBoost come out of the theme and their draw call comes out of the renderer.
2 · A legato pair is one path. Set legatoJoinGap: 0 so the second pill's left edge equals the first's right edge, build the union of the two rounded rects as a single path (legatoSeamOverlap: 1 px of overlap first, to kill the antialiased hairline), fill it per-cell with each note's finger colour, then stroke the outside once — legatoJoinOutline: "union". Radii stay 20px, so the seam self-pinches. A chain of three or more legato notes merges the same way.
3 · Legato ≠ run capsule. The existing 8px run merge still applies to picked notes and keeps its 3px dividers. A legato pair has no divider and no gap. If a legato pair falls inside a run, the divider at that seam is suppressed and the notch takes its place.
4 · Arc anchoring. hammerArcAnchor: "pill-centre" — feet at (x + w/2, pillTop) of each pill, apex at the midpoint of the two centres hammerArcRise above. The letter centres on the apex x, markGap clear of the stroke. Same anchoring for the pull-off — it is one code path.
5 · Hit-testing. noteAt() still needs a rect per note: record the pre-union per-cell rects in lastLayout, split at the seam centre, so click-to-correct-fingering keeps working on a merged pair.
Turn 2 — review of the built stage
The slide stops being a symbol and becomes a contour
Every mock-up below is drawn at the real geometry of a 1080px-tall stage — 164px lanes, 96px pills, 75px sixteenths, the new 9px nick — so what you see is the size it will be. The pill outline, the finger hues, the lane weights and the play line are untouched. 2a is the recommendation; the updated highway-theme.default.json ships it.

2a
Slide, contour rail — recommended
The bird happens because two diagonals of equal weight meet at a point. Take the point away: give each note a short flat plateau at its own pitch level and let the diagonal do nothing but travel between them. A slide down then up now reads as a contour — high, down, low, up, high — which is what the hand actually does. It takes no width from any note, it lives inside the 34px clear lane, and it never crosses the string line above.

2f
What ships, and what needs renderer work
Values are in highway-theme.default.json, with a valueNotes block at the end explaining each new key. highway-theme.daylight.json carries the same keys.
New renderer behaviour
1 · A slide is a chain, not a pair. Walk consecutive slide-linked notes into one chain, assign each note a contour level (start level chosen so the whole chain fits inside slideRailMaxLevels, clamping beyond that), then draw one polyline: plateau over each note inset by slideRailPlateauInset, riser across the nick between levels. slideTailWidth and slideTailAngle are dead and removed.
2 · One rail per shape, not per string. Group simultaneous slide chains that share their start and end times across strings; draw the rail once, above the topmost string of the group. This is the change that fixes 05-full-stage.
3 · The bridge gets a height. The joined pair currently bridges a thin band; make it bridgeStraightRatio × pillHeight centred on the pill. Corner radii unchanged — draw the bridge as a rectangle filled with the pill colour, no keyline on its vertical edges, keyline on its top and bottom.
4 · Chord names may leave the block. Measure the name at fretSizeRatio; shrink only down to chordNameMinSizeRatio; below that, draw it as stage text starting at the block's right edge plus chordNameOverflowGap, vertically centred on the block, cream with the standard halo. Suppress it if the next chord block would collide.
5 · P.M. is per string group. Rail placement moves to the lane above the highest muted string of a run rather than the header band.
Checked and left alone
Pill outline and radii, the four finger hues and the open-string cream, lane weights, bar numbering, the play line and its cap, the harmonic diamond, the hammer-on arc and its H / P, the hit flash. The 7px tail nick became 9px, which is the only change to a settled value — and it is still one uniform subtraction, so every sixteenth remains the same length as every other sixteenth.

Why this holds
Spacing is fixed at 300 px per beat at a 1080 px stage, not at "two bars of look-ahead" — so a sixteenth is always 75 px wide and the number never has to shrink. Where notes abut, they stop being separate pills and become one run capsule with 3 px dividers: the outline stops multiplying, the rhythm still reads as cells, and the 56 px numbers stay at full size. Isolated notes are padded out to 88 px minimum; runs keep their exact musical width. A cell narrower than 56 px (thirty-seconds and faster) drops its number and shows a plain coloured cell — unreadable at that rate anyway, and the score view is one keypress away.

Which one

The change
Today presentation mode recolours the score but keeps alphaTab's default scale, so the tab numbers land around 13 px on the share — unreadable across a room. OBS rules
Three changes, all in the renderer, none in the palette. One — every drawn shape carries a keyline: 2.5 px #0D110B around pills (1.5 px in opaque modes), so a light pill on a light shirt still has an edge. Two — structure lines double up: a 2 px near-white line with a 2 px dark line beneath it. A single mid-tone reads on dark and on light but vanishes against mid-grey, which is exactly what a lot of footage is. Three — header text gets a 3 px dark text shadow rather than a background plate, so it never boxes off part of the camera. Switch the artboard's backdrop with the OBS artboard tweak to check all three.

How they separate
Four hues at roughly 90° apart — lemon, mint, sky, rose — is the widest spread available at this lightness, and hue is only the first cue. The greyscale luminances run 74 → 66 → 52 → 43, a clean descending ladder, so the four stay distinguishable in a deuteranopic read, in a badly compressed Zoom stream, and in a black-and-white screenshot. Every pill clears 6.5:1 with the brand ink — well past 4.5 — because the whole set sits in the top third of the lightness range; that is also what lets one ink colour serve all six pill types, with no luminance branching in the renderer.

F4 · Where I left the design system
The stage keeps four accent hues
Already granted by the build plan. Two of the four — lemon and sky — are new to the palette, not derived from it: the existing sage and plum are too close in lightness to tell apart at speed, which is the failure the brief describes.
Amber appears twice in presentation mode
The play line and the Play button are both amber. They never share a screen in normal mode; in presentation they do. I'd keep it — the play line is not a control, it's the beat — but if you want the rule kept literally, make the presentation Play button sage and leave amber to the line.
Open strings are filled, not hollow
The build plan says ground-coloured fill. That's invisible in OBS, where there is no ground. Paper-2 fill with a 6 px inset ink ring instead — same "open" read, works over anything.
Look-ahead is no longer measured in bars
"≈2 bars" forces sixteenths to 47 px, narrower than a two-digit number. Fixing 300 px per beat gives 1.25 bars at 4/4 and a 75 px sixteenth. Musical spacing stays constant, as specified — only the constant moves.
One shadow became two
The amber glow under the Play button stays the only shadow in the chrome. The stage's keylines and hit rings are drawn strokes on a canvas, not CSS shadows — different medium, different rule.
On the tension in the brief
I agree with your working assumption and haven't tried to make the chrome impressive. What made it read as a prototype wasn't a lack of polish, it was that everything was visible at once. Taking the drop zone out of the song view and giving the transport its own dock does more for "considered and premium" than any new surface treatment would.
